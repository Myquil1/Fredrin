## Work

### Card 1

- Image. https://michaelawilson.co/img/home-03-mileiq-recommendations-screen-iphone-unclass.webp, 1400 by 875
- Image alt. MileIQ Recommendations screen on an iPhone, listing suggested drives with a Business or Personal label on each
- Label. MileIQ, designing for AI
- Title. Reviewing a model that is sometimes wrong
- Body. MileIQ's model could suggest whether a drive was business or personal, and it could be wrong. I was the sole designer on the trust, review, and bulk classification patterns. Every suggestion landed in one reviewable list. The team received a Microsoft Patent Award.
- Result. Classification rate up 10 to 20 percentage points
- Why it's here. The closest thing in my work to a Fredrin review flow. One person checking machine output at volume.

### Card 2

- Image. https://michaelawilson.co/img/home-06-a-d-global-accounts-table-actions-menu.webp, 1400 by 875
- Image alt. A-D Global support portal accounts table with a three dot actions menu open on one row, showing View account, Edit, Duplicate and Archive
- Label. A-D Global, usability testing
- Title. Visible beats clean
- Body. Account managers worked in one data table all day. I was sure that hiding row actions until hover would clean it up. I tested it with five power users and none of them found the action on the first try. One decided the feature had been removed. I shipped a persistent actions column with a three dot menu on every row, and everyone in the next round got it on the first try.
- Result. Across the portal launch, median time on a core flow fell 21 percent and advanced reporting adoption rose 27 percent
- Why it's here. The closest thing in my work to a Fredrin board. A dense screen people keep open all day.
- Link text. Read the case study
- Link. https://michaelawilson.co/a-d-global-case-study

## Principles

Section heading on the page is **How I design dense interfaces**.

| Statement | Supporting line |
| --- | --- |
| Visible beats clean. | For people who live in a tool all day, an action they can see is faster than an interface that looks tidy. I learned that from five users who couldn't find a hidden action. |
| A button beats a prompt when a button works. | Chat is the wrong shape for most actions. When an open prompt replaces clear navigation, people have to learn prompting to do what a click used to do. |
| Every state gets designed. | Empty, loading, error, disabled, focused. At fromAtoB every button was documented in five states. One of my coded prototypes is called [One field, every state](https://michaelawilson.co/one-field). |

## Notes

Section heading on the page is **Notes from building this page in Fredrin**.

| Element | Copy |
| --- | --- |
| Intro | I built this page in Fredrin on November 25, 2026, my first time using it. Here is what I noticed. |
| What worked | Conflicts. Two tickets ran at once and both edited the same part of index.html. The second ticket said Conflicts with main, paused its auto merge, and gave me one button, Fix Conflict. About a minute later it was merged. I never opened a terminal. |
| Quick win 1 | What I saw. Fredrin rewrote my ticket when I created it. The title changed, my section labels changed, and the Activity tab logged the description going from 10,270 characters to 9,663. Why it matters. My brief said to save a block of copy character for character. I couldn't tell what the Worker actually received, so I compared the committed file against my own copy to be sure. What I'd try. Keep the original one click away. A toggle on the ticket summary that switches between what I typed and what the Worker got. |
| Quick win 2 | What I saw. With two tickets running, both cards on the board showed a title and a timestamp. The plan checklist and its count only show after you open the ticket. Why it matters. The brief for this role is to make twenty agents feel as calm as one. I can't open twenty tickets to see which one is stuck. What I'd try. Put the checklist count and the current step on the card, so the board answers how far along a ticket is without a click. The sketch above does this. |
| One observation | The board tells me a ticket's state, and the reason for that state usually sits one level down. A ticket sat under Needs attention while its panel said it would merge itself. A finished run ended its terminal preview with a heartbeat rejected line that read like a failure. I'd bring the reason and the next step up next to the status. The sketch above is my first pass at that. |
