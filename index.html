Notes from building this page in Fredrin

I built this page in Fredrin on November 25, 2026, my first time using it. Here is what I noticed.

What worked

Conflicts. Two tickets ran at once and both edited the same part of index.html. The second ticket said Conflicts with main, paused its auto merge, and gave me one button, Fix Conflict. About a minute later it was merged. I never opened a terminal.

Quick win 1

What I saw.
Fredrin rewrote my ticket when I created it. The title changed, my section labels changed, and the Activity tab logged the description going from 10,270 characters to 9,663.
Why it matters.
My brief said to save a block of copy character for character. I couldn't tell what the Worker actually received, so I compared the committed file against my own copy to be sure.
What I'd try.
Keep the original one click away. A toggle on the ticket summary that switches between what I typed and what the Worker got.

Quick win 2

What I saw.
With two tickets running, both cards on the board showed a title and a timestamp. The plan checklist and its count only show after you open the ticket.
Why it matters.
The brief for this role is to make twenty agents feel as calm as one. I can't open twenty tickets to see which one is stuck.
What I'd try.
Put the checklist count and the current step on the card, so the board answers how far along a ticket is without a click. The sketch above does this.

One observation

The board tells me a ticket's state, and the reason for that state usually sits one level down. A ticket sat under Needs attention while its panel said it would merge itself. A finished run ended its terminal preview with a heartbeat rejected line that read like a failure. I'd bring the reason and the next step up next to the status. The sketch above is my first pass at that.

How I design dense interfaces

  • Visible beats clean.

    For people who live in a tool all day, an action they can see is faster than an interface that looks tidy. I learned that from five users who couldn't find a hidden action.

  • A button beats a prompt when a button works.

    Chat is the wrong shape for most actions. When an open prompt replaces clear navigation, people have to learn prompting to do what a click used to do.

  • Every state gets designed.

    Empty, loading, error, disabled, focused. At fromAtoB every button was documented in five states. One of my coded prototypes is called One field, every state.
